GridShift

2026 · Frozen 2025 evaluation

A more accurate carbon-intensity forecast is useful, but it does not automatically make a scheduler better. GridShift treats those as separate questions.

The question

Electricity carbon intensity varies over time. A flexible job can wait for a lower-intensity window, but a forecast gain is not itself proof of lower emissions.

The approach

The team study compares time-series baselines on frozen 2025 data, then separately evaluates scheduling policies under declared workload and power assumptions. Selection happens on validation data, not after inspecting the test result.

Implementation

  1. Data and provenance

    Official NESO carbon-intensity records are normalized into half-hour intervals with missing observations identified.

  2. Forecast evaluation

    Models share a frozen comparison over common issue origins and endpoint horizons.

  3. Scheduling experiment

    The selected calendar-mean policy is compared with earliest execution on isolated synthetic job cases.

  4. Recorded artifacts

    Structured results, protocol freezes, and a reproducibility guide preserve what was evaluated.

My part in the work

This is a team project and these are team outcomes. I do not claim personal ownership of every model fit, simulation, or result-producing step.

Results and limits

SARIMA reduced the common mean endpoint MAE by 16.03% relative to persistence in the frozen comparison. That metric averages leads of 0.5, 2, 6, 12, and 24 hours.

The separate calendar-mean scheduling policy reduced modeled operational CO2 by 12.41% across 2,913 isolated job cases, with zero deadline misses. The selected scheduling policy was calendar mean, not SARIMA.

What this does not establish

Power, duration, arrivals, and PUE are modeled. No GPU energy or actual avoided grid emissions were measured. The overlapping counterfactual cases are not one executable annual trace.

The results cover frozen 2025 conditions. Retrospective data and unverified historical publication vintages limit stronger causal or deployment claims.
